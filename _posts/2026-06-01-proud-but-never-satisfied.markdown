"You should be proud of yourself... Reaching this far". Indeed, I feel proud, because the journey was not easy. That statement, the feeling, and all the memories that come together are the kind of labels for a track record of small jewels forged by endless questions. The kind of questions that leave dents on your soul.

"Why are you doing this ... You are all over the place ... And you volunteered again for sure ... Don't stretch yourself too thin ..." and many more, they don't pass by without leaving their mark, without shaking every belief, without making the road a bit foggy and definitely more challenging.

Reaching this far, despite the nice tingle of achievement, was never the goal. It was challenging: keep moving through the thick fog of doubt, avoiding pitfalls sneaky enough to strangle bright people, keeping my head low when the dust of ego was all over the place... These challenges, among others, I carry now as medals: proof that I will get what I want... One way or another. "if you achieve your goal: set a bigger goal" may be a valid strategy to use, but mine is more novel than that: enjoy the process too much to an extent that striking a goal becomes part of the process itself. It is about finding your limits and then shattering them, it is about challenging yourself to become better every single day.

Now that I embrace a new journey I have lots to do, many things to learn, new horizons to explore and for sure new challenges to conquer. I am set for new levels of growth, along with the pains that have to be paid to reach that high. Do I have all the tools, knowledge, and experience to reach the next goal? No, but I have the confidence that I will get there, I have the motivation to get there, and I have the grit to do whatever it takes to be the best possible version of myself. There is no goal because there is no rest after achieving the goal: it is an endless loop of being better, doing better and leaving a mark. It is about looking back and being proud of the path I took, the gems I left and the scars I carried with me.

Proud I am for sure, but never satisfied.
