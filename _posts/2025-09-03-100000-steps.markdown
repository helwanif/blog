It was a sunny afternoon, people were relaxing around the start area. Hundreds of kilometers were hanging like medals on people’s bags, telling lots of stories about their owners. In a few minutes, the march was about to start.

A few minutes later I found myself like a small boat being hit by waves on both sides. There were many people. Everyone was there for a reason, everyone had a goal to achieve. The answer that I started repeating along the first steps was: I could and would do this, no matter what.

Kilometer after kilometer, I kept reminding myself why I was doing this as the feeling of being tired sneaked in slowly. After a few hours and halfway through, I told myself: I reached halfway, I reached the peak. Now I would start my descend; my way back. Knowing that the second half was when the real ghosts would manifest themselves. It was where the real challenges were hiding in every step.

"Remember why you are doing this" was my second skin when my first one started to peel off. Keeping my reason floating in my head was the silence that muted every screaming pain. I couldn’t walk upright, but there was only one way out of this, and the only way out was to go through it, no matter what it took, no matter what the price was.

My pace slowed down, my shoulders started to hurt, while my head sank into them seeking refuge. Taking another step felt like walking barefoot on shattered glass: any wrong move and I’d end up suffering even more. Again: there was one way out of this, and that exit ran through the finish line.

Approaching the finish line had never felt so sweet before: all the pain faded away as I crossed the line. I stopped for a few minutes, took a deep breath and let the fact sink in. I had done what people were afraid to even think about. 100,000 steps and I never thought of quitting. It was about surviving the crushing waves of pain and suffering. It was about doing whatever it took to go through the finish line.

"You wouldn’t be the same person once you finished this" was a reality and not just a marketing slogan. I had the physical and mental scars that I carried with me. These were my medals, my proof that I could go the distance, I could bear the pain, I could achieve the goal no matter what.
