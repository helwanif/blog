What does it mean to go back to your roots? To the routes that left deeper marks on your soul than the ones you left on them. How do you feel when the smell of wet rocky tight roads, mixed with scents of many events and marbled by noises too diverse to comprehend, takes you back to years you thought everything was too large to understand, too tall to look at, and too hard to conquer.

Is it the hope that what you left behind would be as enjoyable as you thought it would be? Is it the crumbs of your identity that you left behind that brought you back here, looking to feel more complete?

What will you do, when the long street, once a challenge, is shorter than a thought you entertain along the way? When you look down at a person with compassion and humility, while the memory of looking up at him is still crisp and tangy. Is it really about the places, the roads and the people?

I came here, wanting to collect the pieces I left here of myself, scattered over the places and faces. But I am leaving now knowing that those places and faces are not what I needed to feel more complete. Despite not being able to find these, what I found was more revealing. I came to the conclusion that what I needed, what I was looking for, the missing pieces, are with me all the time.

Feeling complete, feeling rich, is something we do and control by ourselves; the environment can be supportive in enforcing a certain goal, but it is never a prerequisite of it. I don't need a specific place, a certain smell or a specific face to feel that way. What were once too tall, too long or too distant is no more than an impression, one that, after all the challenges, the obstacles and the pitfalls I went through, is a faint ghost that evaporates with the most gentle blow. Now with the skeletons shrinking into toys, time to resume the chase to greatness.
